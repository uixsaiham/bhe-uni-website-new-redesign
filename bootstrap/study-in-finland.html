Why Study in Finland

Finland has research-focused universities and professionally oriented universities of applied sciences (UAS). If you plan to study in Finland, you can explore English-taught degrees in business, computing, engineering, design and other subjects at institutions such as the University of Helsinki, Aalto University and Tampere University.

Different approaches to academic and practical learning

Student life ranges from Helsinki and Espoo to Tampere, Turku and Oulu. Some programmes emphasise independent research; others include applied projects and workplace learning. Review course curricula and teaching language, using university profiles for broader comparisons rather than choosing solely by name or location.

English-taught courses are available, but learning Finnish or Swedish can help with everyday life and employment prospects. Compare course content, tuition fees, language expectations and local costs before deciding. A Finnish qualification does not automatically lead to a job or residence rights.

Types of Study Routes in Finland

Finnish higher education includes different academic and applied pathways. Your choice depends on qualifications, career interests and each programme’s published criteria. You can also compare courses across destinations.

Preparatory and Pathway Studies

May suit students who:

Need academic preparation before entering an eligible degree
Want to develop study skills or language proficiency
Are considering a pathway linked to a Finnish institution

Not every institution offers a pathway, and preparatory study does not guarantee degree entry. Confirm recognition, eligibility and progression directly with the institution.

Bachelor’s Degrees

Popular options include:

University bachelor’s degrees in academic subjects
Applied sciences degrees in business, ICT or engineering
Degree programmes with practical projects or placements

Degree duration depends on the route and institution. Admission may involve academic results, examinations, portfolios or other programme-specific selection criteria.

Master’s Degree Programmes

For graduates seeking advanced study, common options include:

Research-oriented university master’s degrees
Specialist programmes in technology, business and design
UAS master’s degrees requiring relevant work experience

University master’s degrees and UAS master’s degrees have different academic and professional entry routes. Check any previous-study and employment requirements.

Doctoral and Research Degrees

For applicants pursuing original research, options include:

Research-based master’s pathways
Doctoral studies at Finnish research universities

Doctoral study normally requires relevant postgraduate preparation and a research proposal or project. Confirm supervision, funding and admission arrangements with the university.

Basic Entry Requirements for Studying in Finland

Finnish universities and UAS institutions set programme-specific eligibility and selection criteria.

For a bachelor’s programme, you generally need an academic qualification giving access to higher education, such as HSC, A levels or WAEC where accepted. Master’s applicants normally need an appropriate previous degree; universities of applied sciences may also require relevant employment experience for UAS master’s study. Selection can include entrance tests, interviews or other assessment. Always check the programme entry requirements on Studyinfo.fi.

English-taught degree programmes require language evidence that meets their individual rules. An institution may accept IELTS Academic, TOEFL iBT, PTE Academic or specified qualifications or exemptions. Requirements differ, so check the exact programme page before booking a test. BHE UNI can help you compare published criteria and organise your academic records.

Plan for programme tuition fees, accommodation, food, travel and other study expenses. Fee liability, exemptions and any admissions charges depend on the applicant and programme. BHE UNI can explain published academic costs and scholarship terms, but does not offer immigration advice or prepare residence permit applications. For immigration requirements, use the Finnish Immigration Service (Migri) or an appropriately authorised adviser.

Application requirements may include identity details, certificates, transcripts, proof of English and programme-specific attachments. Certain degrees also request portfolios, a CV, motivation statement or references. Check translation and verification rules before submission, and use the university’s instructions alongside the official Finnish admissions guidance.

Costs and Scholarships for Studying in Finland

The cost of studying in Finland combines tuition, living expenses and possible application or student charges. Always check the current figures for your programme.

Costs depend on:

Tuition fees generally apply to non-EU/EEA students on English-taught bachelor’s and master’s programmes, subject to relevant exemptions. Fees differ by course and institution and may change between intakes.

Living costs vary with your location, student accommodation, transport and spending habits. Assess the cost of living in Finland separately from fees and consult the official fees and living costs guidance for updated figures.

BHE UNI supports you by:

Comparing published tuition fees and typical costs in Finnish study cities
Planning a realistic budget without assuming part-time employment income
Reviewing Finnish university scholarships and tuition fee waivers alongside general scholarship information for comparison

Finnish universities and UAS may offer competitive scholarships or tuition reductions, usually focused on fees rather than living costs. Funding is not guaranteed; check the institution’s published terms.

Working While You Study in Finland

Some students in Finland can undertake paid work alongside their studies, subject to the conditions applicable to their individual status. Employment is not guaranteed, and Finnish or Swedish skills may be important for many roles. Plan to meet your study costs without relying on uncertain earnings and check current work-rights information directly with Migri.

Our study-planning guidance can help you:

Locate university career services and information on course placements
Understand study budgets without depending on part-time job income
Balance academic commitments with language and employability skills

After You Graduate

After completing a Finnish degree, graduates may consider employment, postgraduate study or research. Options depend on qualifications, employer requirements and personal circumstances. For any questions about staying in Finland or permission to work, check current official guidance from Migri rather than assuming a degree gives automatic rights.

Frequently Asked Questions about Studying in Finland

01Is Finland a good country for international students?

Finland offers English-taught courses through research universities and universities of applied sciences. Whether it suits you depends on your subject, qualifications, budget, preferred location and willingness to learn Finnish or Swedish.

02What are the entry requirements to study in Finland?

Entry depends on the degree and institution. Bachelor’s programmes generally require a qualification that permits higher education entry; master’s degrees require appropriate previous study. UAS master’s programmes may also require relevant work experience. Check each programme on Studyinfo.fi.

03Can I study in Finland in English without IELTS?

Possibly. English-taught degrees may accept different language tests, previous qualifications or exemptions. IELTS is not universally required, but you must meet the specific programme’s published English language criteria.

04When should I apply to Finnish universities?

Application periods vary by university, programme and intake. Some degrees use joint applications through Studyinfo.fi; others have separate application periods. Start researching well ahead of your planned start and confirm deadlines on the official course page.

05How much does it cost to study in Finland?

Tuition depends on your nationality or fee status, degree and institution. Many non-EU/EEA students pay for English-taught bachelor’s or master’s study. You should also budget independently for accommodation, food and everyday expenses. Check current costs and scholarships with the university.
