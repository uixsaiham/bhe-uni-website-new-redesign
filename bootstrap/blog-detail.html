Can I go to university without A levels?

Dr Mohammad ShafiqDirector, BHE UNI
Updated 22 September 2026 10 min read

Yes, you can go to university without A levels. Depending on the degree, UK universities may accept an Access to HE Diploma, BTEC National qualification, T Level, International Baccalaureate or recognised overseas qualification. Other routes include a foundation year, relevant professional experience or a course with no formal entry requirements.

The important distinction is between having no A levels and having no qualifications. You might already hold an alternative that meets a university’s requirements. If you do not, a preparatory course could help you reach them.

For 2027 entry, start with the degree you want to study. Then work backwards to the qualifications, subjects and grades it accepts. That avoids spending time and money on a route that leads somewhere else.

Do not enrol on a new qualification just because it sounds suitable. Send its full title and awarding body to the admissions team first, together with your intended degree and entry year.

Ways to Get Into University Without A Levels

1. Take an Access to HE Diploma

An Access to Higher Education Diploma helps students prepare for degree-level study, including those returning to education. Providers offer subject pathways such as health, science, humanities and business.

According to the qualification structure published by QAA, the diploma contains 60 credits: 45 graded credits at Level 3 and 15 ungraded credits at Level 2 or Level 3. Courses usually take one year, although part-time options can take longer.

Choose the pathway carefully. A nursing degree might ask for specific science content and a particular combination of Distinction and Merit grades. An unrelated Access diploma will not necessarily meet those conditions.

Ask the university to check the actual modules, not just the words “Access to HE”. Also check any GCSE requirements before paying a deposit.

2. Apply for a degree with a foundation year

A foundation year provides preparation before the first year of a degree. It can suit applicants whose qualifications do not meet direct-entry requirements or whose previous subjects do not match their intended course.

It still has entry requirements. Some programmes consider alternative qualifications or experience; others require specified grades or subjects.

Check whether the foundation year forms part of the degree or belongs to a separate pathway programme. Ask which degree you can progress to, what marks you must achieve and whether progression involves another selection process.

For a degree that normally takes three years, adding a foundation year commonly makes the full route four years. That extra preparation can help, but it also changes your budget and graduation date.

A foundation degree is different: it is a higher education qualification in its own right. Do not confuse it with a foundation year when comparing course pages.

3. Use a BTEC or T Level

A vocational qualification can provide a route into a related degree, but “I have a BTEC” does not give an admissions team enough information. The level, subject, qualification size and grades all matter.

For example, Pearson’s UCAS points table assigns 144 points to an RQF Level 3 BTEC National Extended Diploma at DDD. A National Extended Certificate at Distinction carries 48. Those qualifications occupy the same level but represent different amounts of study.

T Levels combine technical learning with an industry placement in the chosen sector. A university may accept a relevant T Level for some degrees without accepting it for every subject.

If you prefer practical assessment, explore the available vocational study options and their progression routes. Before choosing one, check that your target degree accepts it on its own or identify any additional qualification you need.

4. Apply with the IB or an overseas qualification

You do not need to take British A levels simply because you want to study at a British university. Institutions publish requirements for the International Baccalaureate and qualifications from other education systems.

The details matter. A course might specify an overall IB score and grades in particular Higher Level subjects. For an overseas school-leaving certificate, the university may consider the awarding system, subjects and results when deciding between direct entry and foundation study.

Use the requirements for your qualification and country. A general statement that a university welcomes international students does not establish whether your certificate meets the academic threshold.

5. Ask about experience or an open-entry course

Relevant work can strengthen an application, particularly when a course offers individual assessment. It does not automatically replace the academic knowledge that a degree requires.

For example, someone who supervises staff and manages budgets could explain how those responsibilities prepare them for business study. An admissions tutor may still ask for recent academic work, an interview or a preparatory qualification.

Some courses explicitly remove formal entry requirements. That creates another route, although you still need the time, skills and resources to complete the degree.

If you are returning after a long break, compare the options for restarting education with limited qualifications. Look at the support and assessment methods alongside the admissions policy.

6. Build on an HNC, HND or foundation degree

A Higher National Certificate (HNC), Higher National Diploma (HND) or foundation degree may give you a route beyond the first year of a related degree. Universities assess the qualification level, credits, modules and grades before deciding whether to award advanced entry.

An HND or foundation degree may support an application to a relevant top-up course. However, holding the qualification does not guarantee entry into the final year of every degree.

If you already have one, investigate completing a full bachelor’s degree from your existing qualification before starting again. Have your transcript and module descriptions ready so the admissions team can assess the academic match.

7. Consider a degree apprenticeship

A degree apprenticeship combines paid employment with university study. You earn a salary while working towards a degree, and UCAS explains that the degree tuition is paid for.

Entry requirements vary by vacancy. Look for employers and university partners that explicitly accept your alternative qualification; this is not a route that automatically removes academic requirements.

You apply for an advertised job rather than relying on the standard UCAS undergraduate deadline. Check the employer’s closing date and eligibility conditions, and be ready to explain why you want both the role and the degree.

What Entry Requirements Still Apply?

University without A levels does not mean university without standards. Even when a provider accepts an alternative route, a course may require particular subjects, GCSE English or maths, evidence of English proficiency, a portfolio, an audition or an interview. Professional courses can have additional selection requirements.

UCAS explains that requirements vary by course and provider. Read the full course page, including the section for alternative qualifications, rather than relying on its headline offer.

More UCAS points do not always mean a better match. A qualification with no Tariff points can still be acceptable, while one with plenty of points can be unsuitable for your chosen degree. The Tariff guidance makes clear that points do not determine every admissions decision.

Think about it this way: 144 points cannot replace a required subject if your qualification does not cover it.

Where your results fall below a standard offer, you can compare courses with more flexible entry thresholds. Check subject requirements first, then consider the grades.

The useful question is not simply, “Does this university accept students without A levels?” Ask, “Does this course accept my exact qualification, subjects and grades for 2027 entry?”

Keep the admissions team’s written reply. It gives you something concrete to use when deciding whether to apply or take further study.

Can International Students Apply Without A Levels?

Yes. The key question is whether the university accepts your existing qualification for the course you want.

A recognised overseas school-leaving certificate may support direct entry. If it does not provide the required preparation, the university may suggest a foundation programme or another qualification. Do not assume that every international applicant needs a foundation year.

Send admissions your certificate, transcript and qualification details. Ask separately about academic eligibility and the evidence of English the university accepts. A satisfactory academic result does not automatically satisfy an English-language condition.

Also check who can enrol on the proposed pathway. A route available to a UK resident may have different arrangements for an applicant studying overseas or planning to move to the UK.

Before committing, get confirmation of the course, study location, available start date and any progression conditions. That is more useful than a general promise that a qualification “leads to university”.

How to Get Into University Without A Levels for 2027 Entry

Start by shortlisting degrees that match both your intended career and your current qualifications. Then check the entry requirements for each course individually.

When an alternative qualification does not appear on the course page, email admissions before submitting an application. Include its full name, awarding body, subjects, grades or predicted results, and completion date. For experience-based consideration, explain the relevant responsibilities you have held.

Once the university confirms a viable route, gather your documents and begin preparing the UCAS application. Use the current personal statement guidance to explain your motivation and preparation. Specific evidence works better than broad claims about being passionate or hardworking.

For example, a care worker applying for a health-related degree could discuss relevant training and what it taught them about communication or professional responsibility. They should still address the course’s academic requirements.

The official UCAS timetable sets these key dates for 2027 entry:

  • 15 October 2026, 18:00 UK time: the deadline for Oxford and Cambridge, and most medicine, dentistry and veterinary medicine or science courses.
  • 13 January 2027, 18:00 UK time: the equal consideration deadline for most undergraduate courses.

A January application deadline does not mean a January course start. If you need an earlier start, check the specific courses available in January. Otherwise, build your application schedule around the autumn entry timetable.

If you need to complete an Access diploma or another preparatory qualification first, check whether you can finish it before the degree begins. Let that determine your application year.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I go to university with no A levels but with GCSEs?

Possibly, but GCSEs alone do not normally meet direct-entry requirements for a conventional bachelor’s degree. You may need further preparation, such as an Access diploma or an appropriate foundation route. An explicitly open-entry course offers another possibility. Check the entry conditions of the preparatory course too, particularly English and maths requirements.

Can I get into university without A levels if I have work experience?

Some providers consider relevant experience, especially through individual assessment for mature applicants. Explain what you have done and how it relates to the subject. Experience may support your case without replacing every academic requirement, so ask whether the course also needs recent study, particular qualifications or an interview.

Can I go to university without any qualifications at all?

Some courses have no formal entry requirements, but that is different from saying that any degree accepts applicants without qualifications. You may need to build academic skills before starting. If this describes your position, explore routes for applicants without recognised certificates and confirm the requirements of the specific course.

Is an Access course better than a foundation year?

Neither is automatically better. An Access diploma can prepare you to apply for suitable degrees at different institutions, subject to their requirements. A foundation year usually connects more directly to a particular degree or group of degrees. Compare subject coverage, progression conditions, cost and the places where you want to study before choosing.

Can I study medicine without A levels?

Potentially, through an accepted alternative qualification or a specifically eligible pathway. However, medical schools set detailed requirements, and acceptance of an Access diploma, foundation programme or other qualification varies. Never assume that a general health or science pathway qualifies you for medicine. Obtain confirmation from each medical school before enrolling on preparatory study.

Will Clearing let me apply without A levels?

Clearing can help you find courses with vacancies, but it does not remove their entry requirements. A university may consider your alternative qualification or review your results for an available place. Contact the provider with your exact qualifications and grades; do not treat Clearing as a guaranteed substitute for meeting academic conditions.

Do I need a specific number of UCAS points?

Only if your chosen course uses a Tariff-based offer. Some universities specify qualification grades or subjects instead, and some acceptable qualifications do not carry Tariff points. Even where a points total appears, check whether the university requires those points to come from particular qualifications or subjects.

Find the Route That Matches Your Starting Point

You do not need A levels for every UK university degree. You do need a route that your chosen course accepts and a realistic plan for completing it.

If you are unsure whether to apply directly or take further study, discuss your qualifications and preferred course. Have your results, subject choice and intended start date ready so the next step can focus on your actual options.
