Study in Spain

for international students

Why Study in Spain

Spain has established public and private universities offering business, engineering, health sciences, technology and the arts. When you study in Spain, institutions such as the University of Barcelona, Complutense University of Madrid and Pompeu Fabra University illustrate the range of academic choices.

European qualifications, different languages and study environments

Spain follows the European Higher Education Area framework, with bachelor’s, master’s and doctoral degrees. Student life varies from Madrid and Barcelona to Valencia, Granada and Salamanca. Teaching is often in Spanish, although selected courses use English or other languages. Check each programme’s teaching language.

Spanish degrees can offer academic and international experience. Entry, recognition and career prospects vary by programme. Check whether a qualification is officially registered in Spain’s RUCT, and compare living expenses before choosing a university. Check SEPIE’s Spanish degree-search tools when comparing programmes.

If you are considering higher education in Spain, this page explains the main academic pathways and questions to check before applying.

Types of Study Routes in Spain

Spanish higher education offers preparatory, undergraduate, postgraduate and research routes. The right choice depends on your previous qualifications and each university’s admission criteria.

Foundation and Preparatory Routes

May suit applicants who:

Need to strengthen Spanish language or academic readiness
Want preparation for a university-specific entry assessment
Are checking whether previous qualifications meet admission rules

Preparatory courses do not guarantee admission. Confirm their provider, progression criteria and recognition by your target university.

Undergraduate Degrees (Grado)

Typical study choices include:

Official bachelor’s degrees at public universities
Bachelor’s courses at recognised private universities
Programmes in business, engineering, science or the arts

Official bachelor’s degrees commonly carry 240 ECTS credits, with exceptions. International entry may require UNEDasiss, subject tests or qualification recognition.

Postgraduate Master’s Degrees

For graduates wishing to specialise, options include:

Official university master’s degrees (Máster Universitario)
Specialist postgraduate programmes taught in English or Spanish
Eligible Erasmus Mundus joint master’s courses

Official master’s programmes generally carry 60–120 ECTS credits. Verify whether a qualification is official or university-specific before applying.

Doctoral and Research Degrees

For applicants pursuing research, routes include:

Doctoral programmes (Doctorado) at Spanish universities
Research projects supported by academic supervisors

Doctoral admission normally requires relevant prior higher education and programme-specific eligibility. Discuss research fit, supervisory availability and possible funding with the institution.

Basic Entry Requirements for Studying in Spain

Spanish university entry requirements vary by degree, country of qualification and institution.

For a bachelor’s degree in Spain, some foreign applicants need school qualification recognition (homologación), UNEDasiss accreditation or PCE examinations, depending on their university and background. Master’s applicants generally need a suitable previous degree.

Many universities teach primarily in Spanish. Selected English-taught courses in Spain may accept IELTS, TOEFL or other language evidence; Spanish-language courses may require DELE, SIELE or equivalent proof. Check each programme’s actual teaching language and accepted tests or exemptions.

Budget for tuition, accommodation, food and travel. Public fees vary by region, course and student category; private fees are institution-specific. BHE UNI offers education and budget information, not immigration advice or visa, residence or student-finance application services. For entry and stay requirements, consult Spain’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs or an appropriately authorised adviser.

Universities may request identity details, certificates, transcripts, language results and course-specific documents such as a CV, portfolio or references. Some require translations, legalisation or academic recognition. Check the university’s instructions and UNEDasiss guidance for international applicants before submitting academic documents.

Costs and Scholarships for Studying in Spain

The cost of studying in Spain depends on the university, subject, location and your individual fee category.

Costs depend on:

Tuition fees at public universities depend on regional rules, course level and student category. Private universities set their own charges. Compare official prices for your particular programme.

Living costs vary by accommodation, transport, food and city. Madrid and Barcelona may cost more than smaller university towns. Consult European Education Area’s Spain study profile and local university estimates before budgeting.

BHE UNI supports you by:

Comparing published fees for public and private universities in Spain
Building a realistic study budget without relying on uncertain job income
Identifying university scholarships and eligible European funding schemes

University scholarships, regional awards and Erasmus Mundus funding have different eligibility rules. Support is competitive and is never guaranteed.

Working While You Study in Spain

Some international students may work during their studies, subject to their individual permission and conditions. Work is not guaranteed, and Spanish language skills can affect opportunities. Budget without relying on earnings, and check current rules with the relevant authority or an authorised adviser.

Our study-planning guidance can help you:

Find university career centres and information on academic placements
Compare living costs without depending on part-time earnings
Consider language learning alongside study and employability goals

After You Graduate

A Spanish qualification may support further study, research or professional development. Opportunities depend on your degree, language skills and experience. Check official Spanish guidance about any permission to stay or work; graduation alone guarantees neither employment nor residence rights.

Frequently Asked Questions About Studying in Spain

01Is Spain a good place to study for international students?

Spain has public and private universities, official degree pathways and both Spanish-taught and selected English-taught courses. Suitability depends on the programme, your language skills, budget, academic background and preferred city.

02What are the requirements to study in Spain?

Entry requirements vary by university, degree and previous education. Some international bachelor’s applicants need qualification recognition, UNEDasiss accreditation or entrance assessments. Master’s applicants normally need a suitable previous degree. Check the university’s own criteria.

03Can I study in Spain in English without IELTS?

Yes, some universities offer English-taught programmes and may accept alternative proof of English or specified exemptions. Other programmes require Spanish. IELTS is not universally mandatory, but you must meet the exact language conditions of your chosen course.

04How much does it cost to study in Spain?

Tuition fees differ between public and private universities, degree levels and student categories. Living costs also vary by city and accommodation. Compare the current university fee schedule and your personal budget, rather than relying on one general figure.

05Are scholarships available to study in Spain?

Some universities offer scholarships or fee reductions, and eligible students may explore regional or European schemes such as Erasmus Mundus joint master’s funding. Eligibility, value and deadlines depend on the award. No scholarship is guaranteed.
